## 1. When + why

- **Fly out Fri 27 Aug, land Sat 28 Aug 10:50, fly home Fri 10 Sep. 13 nights: Copenhagen 3, Oslo 2, Aurland 2, Bergen 3, Stavanger 3.** Twelve would drop the Lysefjord/Stavanger buffer day (the one weather slack in the plan); fourteen adds a Hardanger or Sognefjord detour that costs a full transit day for one more fjord — not worth it.
- Why this week: (a) **Weekday-locked buses.** In September the Aurlandsdalen hiking bus runs only Fri/Sat/Sun ([Norway's Best](https://www.norwaysbest.com/en/flam/things-to-do/bus-aurlandsdalen)) and the Kjerag bus only Sat/Sun ([Go Fjords](https://gofjords.com/experiences/hiking/stavanger/kjerag-express-bus-round-trip/)); this calendar puts Aurlandsdalen on **Fri 3 Sep**. (b) **Bergen Matfestival** (West Norway food market, Michelin seafood contest, cider festival) was Thu–Sat 3–5 Sep 2026 ([matfest.no](https://matfest.no/bergen-matfestival/)); 2027 est. Thu 2–Sat 4 Sep — you arrive Bergen Sat 4 Sep afternoon for the last day (verify when 2027 dates publish, ~spring 2027). (c) Schools back in both countries mid-August: Nærøyfjord boats, Flåmsbana and Preikestolen off summer peak while all summer transport still runs to ~mid-Sep. (d) Weather: Copenhagen Sept 17.5/10.8 °C, 61 mm; Bergen Sept 16.1/9.9 °C but **248 mm over 18 wet days** ([Wikipedia climate tables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen)) — pack a real shell; late Aug is marginally drier than mid-Sep. Daylight Bergen ~06:30–20:30 (est.).
- **±1 week**: **+1 week (3–17 Sep)** catches Copenhagen's Golden Days history festival (2026 theme "Monster"; runs ~first three weeks of Sept, dates not yet posted — [goldendays.dk](https://goldendays.dk/), [visitcopenhagen](https://www.visitcopenhagen.com/copenhagen/planning/golden-days-festival-gdk1091865)) and puts Stavanger on a weekend (Kjerag bus), but loses Matfestival, AC828 shifts to Mon/Wed/Thu/Sat only after ~13 Sep ([flightmapper](https://info.flightmapper.net/flight/Air_Canada_AC_828)), and Preikestolen buses drop to two morning departures ([preikestolen365](https://preikestolen365.com/how-to-get-to-preikestolen/)). **−1 week (20 Aug–3 Sep)** is warmer and busier, misses Matfestival and Aurlandsdalen falls on a weekday with no bus. Stay put.

## 6. Food shortlist (10)

1. **Schønnemann**, Copenhagen — smørrebrød institution since 1877; ~DKK 130–160/piece; herring "Madsen", roast beef; **skip mayonnaise-heavy chicken salad** (dairy-light otherwise). Book 2–3 weeks.
2. **Torvehallerne**, CPH — market; Hallernes smørrebrød ~DKK 85/piece, Grød (oat porridge with oat milk), Coffee Collective. Walk in.
3. **Aamanns 1921**, CPH — modern smørrebrød + natural wine; ~DKK 350–450; ask for dishes without crème fraîche.
4. **Reffen**, CPH — 30+ stalls, ~DKK 100–150; check Sept opening days.
5. **Vippa**, Oslo — harbour food hall; Syrian, Eritrean, fish; ~NOK 150–200; easy dairy-free.
6. **Mathallen Oslo** — Vulkan market hall; Vulkanfisk fish soup (cream) or fish cakes; ~NOK 200.
7. **Ægir Brewpub**, Flåm — Viking platter, house beers; ~NOK 450.
8. **Pingvinen**, Bergen — home-style Norwegian: persetorsk, raspeballer, lamb; ~NOK 300–380; sauces on the side.
9. **Bergen Fisketorget / Fjellskål** — grilled halibut, shrimp, whale is on menus (your call); ~NOK 250–400; fish soup is cream-based.
10. **RE-NAA**, Stavanger (splurge) — 3★ tasting ~NOK 3,500; warn kitchen of lactose intolerance at booking (they adapt). Modern alternatives: **Lysverket** Bergen (~NOK 1,800) and **Kontrast** Oslo (1★, ~NOK 2,500).

**Dairy notes:** Danish cuisine leans on butter, remoulade and crème fraîche; Norwegian on brown cheese, rømme (sour cream), fish-soup cream and cream sauces. Skyr is high-lactose. Lactose-free (laktosefri) milk/yoghurt is in every Rema 1000/Kiwi/Netto — grocery breakfasts (rugbrød, knekkebrød, mackerel in tomato, eggs, berries) are the lean lever: Norway restaurant mains run NOK 250–400 (C$37–60), beer NOK 110+.
